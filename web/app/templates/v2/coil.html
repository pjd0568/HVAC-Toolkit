Coil application notes

Why ADP + leaving dry bulb. A simple cooling coil cannot set temperature and moisture independently. Once air-on and the apparatus dew point (mean coil surface temperature) are fixed, the process is the straight line from air-on to ADP on the saturation curve. Specifying off-coil dry bulb only chooses how far along that chord the mixture leaves:

BF = (T₂ − TADP) / (T₁ − TADP)

Off-coil RH. Because the chord sits slightly above the curved saturation line, leaving air is rarely at 100% RH — typically ~90–95% for a wet 4–6 row coil. That RH is a result of the geometry, not a free design knob.

Contact and bypass factor. Contact factor is the fraction of air treated toward the ADP; bypass factor is the fraction that streams through closer to air-on. Deep coils / low face velocity raise contact factor; shallow high-velocity coils bypass more.

Frost risk. An ADP at or below 0 °C means the coil surface can freeze condensate. Options: raise chilled water temperature or ADP, glycol, a defrost strategy on DX, or face-and-bypass control.

Face velocity. Keep coil face velocity in the region of 2–2.5 m/s; above ~2.75 m/s condensate carry-over from the fins becomes likely and an eliminator or larger coil face is needed.

Condensate. Size the drain and trap for the calculated rate; the trap depth must exceed the fan pressure at the drain pan.
