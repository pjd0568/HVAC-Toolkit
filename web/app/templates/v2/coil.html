Coil application notes

ADP from the fluid. Apparatus dew point is the mean coil-surface temperature. Estimate it as chilled-water flow temperature (or DX evaporating temperature) plus an approach. Duties use the ADP / bypass-factor end state.

Wet vs dry. If ADP is below the entering dew point, condensate forms. The chart path stays nearly constant-moisture at first, then drops more steeply toward the off-coil state near saturation (bypass holds it short of the ADP). If ADP is at or above the dew point the coil is dry — straight sensible cooling at constant moisture.

BF = (T₂ − TADP) / (T₁ − TADP)

Contact and bypass factor. Contact factor is the fraction of air treated toward the ADP; bypass factor is the fraction that streams through closer to air-on. Deep coils / low face velocity raise contact factor.

Frost risk. An ADP at or below 0 °C means the coil surface can freeze condensate. Options: raise chilled water temperature or ADP, glycol, a defrost strategy on DX, or face-and-bypass control.
