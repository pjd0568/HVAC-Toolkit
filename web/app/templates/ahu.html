Design method & notes

What this does. You give the condition the unit must deliver into the room (the supply, or off-unit, condition); the tool mixes outdoor and return air to the on-coil condition, then works out the coil, reheat or humidification needed to get there. Outdoor air is entered as a volume flow; the fresh-air fraction is derived from dry-air mass flows.

Summer path. Where dehumidification is needed (supply drier than on-coil), the cooling coil leaves air at the supply moisture content and the off-coil RH target (default 90%). A reheat coil then lifts the air sensibly to the supply dry bulb when needed.

Winter path. Where the supply is wetter than on-coil (typical with cold dry outdoor air), the tool sizes a heating coil then isothermal steam humidification — the same sequence as the Heating & Humidification tool.

Sensible-only. If supply moisture equals on-coil moisture the duty collapses to a purely sensible cool or heat.

Frost. An off-coil temperature at or below 0 °C means the coil surface can freeze condensate — consider glycol, a DX defrost strategy or face-and-bypass.

Fan position. A blow-through fan raises the on-coil condition (adding to coil duty); a draw-through fan sits after the last coil/humidifier, so the heating coil targets the supply dry bulb minus the fan rise. Absorbed power can be estimated from specific fan power: P (W) = SFP (W per l/s) × flow (l/s). Approved Document L Volume 2 (England, 2021) limits: central balanced with heating and cooling 2.0 W/(l/s); heating only 1.9; other central balanced 1.5; zonal supply 1.1; zonal extract 0.5.
