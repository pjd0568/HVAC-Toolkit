Design method & notes

What this does. You give the condition the unit must deliver into the room (the supply, or off-unit, condition); the tool mixes outdoor and return air to the on-coil condition, checks the supply is reachable, then works out the coil and reheat needed to get there. It is the inverse of specifying the off-coil condition and reading off the result.

Coil model. Where dehumidification is needed, the cooling coil is taken to leave air at the supply moisture content and the off-coil RH target (default 90%, matching typical wet-coil practice and the Cooling Coil tool). Set the target to 100% for the idealised CF = 1 case (saturated at the supply dew point). Real coils leave air a little off saturation (~90–95% RH). A reheat coil then lifts the air sensibly to the supply dry bulb when the off-coil temperature is below the supply dry bulb.

Reachability. A cooling coil can only remove moisture, so a supply moisture content above the on-coil value is rejected — that duty needs humidification. If the supply moisture equals the on-coil moisture the duty collapses to a purely sensible cool or heat.

Frost. An off-coil temperature (supply dew point) at or below 0 °C means the coil surface can freeze condensate — consider glycol, a DX defrost strategy or face-and-bypass.

Fan position. A blow-through fan raises the on-coil condition (adding to coil duty); a draw-through fan sits after reheat, so the reheat coil targets the supply dry bulb minus the fan rise and the air reaches the supply temperature after the fan. Absorbed power can be estimated from specific fan power: P (W) = SFP (W per l/s) × flow (l/s). Approved Document L Volume 2 (England, 2021) limits: central balanced with heating and cooling 2.0 W/(l/s); heating only 1.9; other central balanced 1.5; zonal supply 1.1; zonal extract 0.5.
