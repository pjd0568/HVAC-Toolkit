Coil application notes

Off-coil RH. A wet (dehumidifying) coil typically leaves air at 90–95% RH; 0.90 is a reasonable design assumption for 4–6 row coils. A higher value implies more coil surface (more rows / closer fins) for the same duty.

ADP, contact and bypass factor. The apparatus dew point is where the extended process line meets the saturation curve — effectively the mean coil surface temperature. The contact factor is the fraction of air treated to the ADP; the bypass factor is the fraction passing through unchanged. Deep coils have high contact factors; shallow high-velocity coils bypass more.

Frost risk. An ADP at or below 0 °C means the coil surface can freeze condensate. Options: raise the chilled water temperature or off-coil target, glycol, a defrost strategy on DX, or face-and-bypass control.

Face velocity. Keep coil face velocity in the region of 2–2.5 m/s; above ~2.75 m/s condensate carry-over from the fins becomes likely and an eliminator or larger coil face is needed.

Condensate. Size the drain and trap for the calculated rate; the trap depth must exceed the fan pressure at the drain pan.
